Standard push-up — balanced chest, triceps, shoulders, core.
Knee push-up — easier version for building strength toward full push-ups.
Incline push-up — hands on a bench/table; easier and emphasizes the lower chest slightly.
Decline push-up — feet elevated; harder, with more upper-chest and shoulder involvement.
Wide-grip push-up — hands wider than shoulders; more chest emphasis.
Close-grip / diamond push-up — hands close together; much more triceps emphasis.
Staggered push-up — one hand ahead of the other; adds uneven loading and core stabilization.
Archer push-up — shift most of your weight toward one arm; a progression toward one-arm push-ups.
One-arm push-up — advanced unilateral chest, triceps, shoulder, and core exercise.
Pike push-up — hips high, body shaped like an inverted V; focuses heavily on shoulders.
Handstand push-up — advanced vertical pressing variation, mainly shoulders and triceps.
Spiderman push-up — bring one knee toward the same-side elbow while lowering; adds oblique and hip work.
Mountain-climber push-up — mix push-ups with alternating knee drives for conditioning and core work.
T push-up — rotate into a side plank after each rep; adds shoulders and rotational core stability.
Clap / plyometric push-up — explode off the floor; develops upper-body power.
Explosive push-up — hands leave the floor without necessarily clapping; a simpler plyometric progression.
Pseudo-planche push-up — hands near the hips and body leaning forward; significantly increases shoulder loading.
Hindu push-up — flowing movement through a pike-like position; works shoulders, chest, triceps, and mobility.
Dive-bomber push-up — similar to Hindu push-ups but reverses through the movement.
Tempo push-up — use a slow lowering phase or pauses; makes regular push-ups considerably harder without extra weight.

For a simple progression, I'd go incline → standard → diamond/decline → archer → one-arm, while using pike → handstand push-up as a separate shoulder-focused progression.

## Speed

Normal tempo: about 1–2 sec down, 1 sec up. Good all-round strength and hypertrophy.
Slow eccentric: 3–5 sec lowering, then push up normally. Great for muscle growth, control, and making bodyweight reps harder.
Paused push-up: lower, pause 1–3 sec near the bottom, then press up. Builds strength out of the hardest position.
Slow both ways: e.g. 4 sec down, 4 sec up. High time-under-tension and very demanding even with fewer reps.
Explosive concentric: controlled descent, then push up as fast as possible. Good for power and athletic pressing strength.
Plyometric: explode hard enough for the hands to leave the floor. More power-focused than ordinary fast reps.
Fast continuous reps: quick down-and-up reps with no pause. Better for muscular endurance and conditioning, assuming form stays solid.
Speed reps with control: descend quickly but under control, then immediately explode upward. Useful for developing rate of force production.
1½ reps: down → halfway up → back down → all the way up. Not strictly a speed variation, but it greatly increases time under tension.
Isometric holds: stop halfway or near the bottom and hold for 5–30 seconds. Builds positional strength and endurance.

A useful tempo notation is 3-1-X-0: 3 sec down → 1 sec pause → explosive up → no pause at the top. The X means "as fast as possible."

For different goals: muscle growth: 3–4 sec down; strength/control: pauses + slow lowering; power: controlled down + explosive up; endurance: faster continuous reps.
